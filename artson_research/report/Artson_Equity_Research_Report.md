# SECTION 1: COMPANY OVERVIEW

## What is Artson Limited?

Artson Limited is a Hyderabad-headquartered Engineering, Manufacturing, and Construction (EMC) contracting company with a 46-year operating history. It is best described as a **specialist EPC/EMC contractor for industrial clients** — primarily in oil, gas, petrochemicals, power, airports, shipbuilding, and heavy industries.

In plain language: Artson builds things that industrial companies need — storage tanks, pressure vessels, piping systems, structural steel, and complete EPC packages — for some of India's most capital-intensive industries.

## Business Overview

Artson operates through two primary modes:

**1. EPC / Construction (Project Execution):**
Design, procure, and construct industrial assets for clients on a fixed-price or cost-plus basis. Revenues are recognized as project milestones are completed. Artson serves as a subcontractor to Tata Projects and also wins independent orders.

**2. Manufacturing (Equipment Supply):**
Manufactures pressure vessels, heat exchangers, columns, reactors, and process equipment at its Nashik facility under ASME U/U2 stamps. This is a more stable, margin-accretive business.

In FY24, for the **first time**, manufacturing contributed more to revenue than EPC — a positive structural shift.

### Contract Structure

Artson's EPC contracts follow a standard project lifecycle:

1. **Tender Stage:** Artson bids on tenders from Tata Projects, PSUs, private sector refiners, airport operators, and industrial companies.
2. **Contract Award:** Fixed-price lump-sum (majority) or item-rate (minority). Contract values range from ₹5 Cr to ₹75+ Cr per project.
3. **Milestone Billing:** Revenue recognized on percentage completion (Ind-AS 115 / POC method). Invoices raised at defined milestones — usually 20–30% advance, progress claims at 30–70%, final 10–20% against retention.
4. **Retention Money:** Typically 5–10% of contract value withheld by clients until defect liability period (6–24 months post-completion). This can trap cash for years.
5. **Working Capital:** Artson uses working capital loans (secured against receivables and project advances) and unsecured loans from Tata Projects to fund operations.

> **Note:** Artson received ₹28.94 Cr from Tata Projects (Dross Refining), ₹25.19 Cr for AMNS fabrication, ₹4.62 Cr order enhancement — all from TPL in recent periods. Dependency on parent for orders is significant and undisclosed precisely.

### Billing & Working Capital Cycle

In EPC businesses, cash flow management is existential. Artson's typical cycle:

- **Advance received:** 10–20% upfront (good)
- **Progress billing:** Monthly/quarterly against milestones
- **Retention held by clients:** 5–10% for 12–24 months (cash locked)
- **Vendor/sub-contractor payments:** 45–90 days after Artson's receipt
- **Working capital gap:** Project sites consume cash before billing catches up

**FY26 concern:** A provision of ₹525 lakhs was made for long-outstanding receivables and unbilled revenue of ₹1,633 lakhs from a **single PSU customer** — this is a major red flag. One bad receivable of ₹16.33 Cr wiped out nearly all the FY25 profit via provision in FY26.

# SECTION 3: PRODUCT AND SERVICE DEEP DIVE

## 3.1 Industrial Storage Tanks (Tankage) — Core Business

**What it is:** Above-ground large-diameter storage tanks (API 650 standard) for petroleum products, chemicals, and other bulk liquids. Typical sizes: 10,000 KL to 100,000 KL.

**Where used:** Petroleum terminals, refineries, petrochemical complexes, airport fuel farms, LNG facilities.

**Execution:** Site-built, using plates and structural steel. Artson supplies materials, fabricates, and constructs on client sites.

**Customers:** IOC, HPCL, BPCL, Air India (SATCOM), GMR Airports, Sadhav Offshore, various port operators.

**Margin Profile:** EBITDA 6–10%. Material-intensive. Execution risk high (site conditions, weather). Long duration (6–18 months per tank).

**Key Risks:** RM price inflation (steel plates), site delays, client payment defaults.

**Growth Triggers:** India's strategic petroleum reserve expansion; airport fuel infrastructure (UDAN scheme); LNG terminal build-out; ethanol/biofuel blending infrastructure.

---

## 3.2 Process Equipment Manufacturing (Pressure Vessels, Heat Exchangers)

**What it is:** Shop-manufactured pressure vessels (ASME U stamp), heat exchangers, columns, reactors, and custom process equipment.

**Where used:** Refineries, petrochemical plants, fertilizer units, power plants, pharmaceutical.

**Execution:** Manufactured at Nashik facility. ASME U/U2 stamp certification allows export of pressure vessels to international clients.

**Customers:** Deepak Chem Tech (₹5.40 Cr order confirmed), refineries, EPC contractors.

**Margin Profile:** Superior to EPC — EBITDA 10–15% range when utilization is healthy. Less weather/site risk.

**Competitive Intensity:** Faces competition from ISGEC, GMM Pfaudler, HLE Glascoat, Alfa Laval India, smaller MSME fabricators.

**Key Risks:** Utilization dependency; raw material (stainless, alloy steel) prices; skilled welder availability.

**Growth Triggers:** India's refinery/petrochemical capacity expansion (11 new refineries planned under Vision 2047); export orders; hydrogen equipment demand.

---

## 3.3 Structural Steel Fabrication

**What it is:** Fabrication of structural steel components — columns, beams, trusses, platforms, pipe racks — for industrial plants and buildings.

**Where used:** Power plants, steel mills, industrial plants, data centres, warehouses.

**Execution:** Mix of shop fabrication (Nashik) and site erection. AMNS (ArcelorMittal Nippon Steel) project worth ₹25.19 Cr for structural fabrication confirmed in FY24.

**Margin Profile:** EBITDA 6–9%. Competitive and commoditized. Scale matters.

**Key Risks:** Steel price volatility; competition from Jindal-group companies, L&T, smaller fabricators.

---

## 3.4 Industrial Piping

**What it is:** Engineering and installation of industrial piping systems for process plants. Includes design, procurement, fabrication, and installation of piping.

**Where used:** Refineries, petrochemical plants, power plants, chemical plants.

**Margin Profile:** EBITDA 8–12% when well-managed. Risk of cost overruns on site.

# SECTION 5: TATA GROUP ANGLE

## 5.1 Structure of the Relationship

Tata Projects Limited (TPL) holds **75.0% of Artson Limited** — a controlling promoter stake. Artson is classified as a subsidiary of TPL.

Tata Projects itself is unlisted and is owned by:
- Tata Sons Limited (~78%)
- Other Tata trusts and entities (~22%)

So the **effective Tata Group → Tata Sons → Tata Projects → Artson** chain is:

```
Tata Sons (Unlisted)
    ↓
Tata Projects Limited (Unlisted, ~₹8,000 Cr revenue)
    ↓ 75% stake
Artson Limited (Listed on BSE/NSE)
    ↓ 100% subsidiary operations (mostly)
```

## 5.2 What the Tata Relationship Provides

| Benefit | Reality Check |
|---|---|
| **Order Flow:** Artson receives subcontracts from TPL | Confirmed: ₹28.94 Cr (Dross Refining), ₹25.19 Cr (AMNS), ₹4.62 Cr enhancement — but total undisclosed |
| **Financial Support:** TPL provides "letter of support" | Critical: Without this, Artson would have been insolvent during FY18–FY22. Going concern basis |
| **Brand Credibility:** "A Tata Enterprise" branding | Real commercial advantage — helps win PSU tenders |
| **Technical Upgrades:** Shared Tata Project practices, policies | Operational improvement evident post-2022 |
| **Slump Sale Support:** Nagpur Division sold to TPL (₹22.60 Cr) | One-time boost — enabled FY25 profit only because of OI |
| **Co-location:** Artson now co-located with TPL | Enhanced collaboration, supply chain resilience |

## 5.3 Strategic Importance of Artson to Tata Projects

TPL's rationale for maintaining Artson:
1. **Supply chain resilience:** Artson provides in-house fabrication/construction capability
2. **Prequalified vendor:** ASME-certified manufacturer that can be deployed in TPL projects
3. **Listed entity:** Provides exit liquidity optionality for TPL in future
4. **Niche expertise:** Tankage, pressure vessels, piping — not TPL's primary business

**Is Artson strategically vital to Tata Projects?** Marginally. TPL's revenue (₹8,000+ Cr) dwarfs Artson (₹164 Cr). Artson is a niche supplier, not a core asset.

## 5.4 Is the Tata Connection a Real Moat?

**Yes, partially:**
- Brand halo helps win PSU tenders
- Access to order flow via TPL
- Financial backstop prevents insolvency
- Co-location improves execution

**No, in important ways:**
- No formal agreement ensuring order flow from TPL
- Related-party dependency creates governance risks
- TPL can redirect orders elsewhere if needed
- Artson's survival depends on TPL's goodwill, not contractual rights
- **If TPL withdraws its "letter of support," Artson faces going concern risk**

### Flag 1: Persistent Operating Losses — SEVERITY: CRITICAL

### Flag 2: Negative Net Worth for Six Consecutive Years — SEVERITY: CRITICAL

**Finding:** Net worth was negative from approximately FY19 through FY24. Accumulated losses exceeded paid-up capital + reserves.

**What happened:** Total PAT losses from FY17 to FY22 = approximately **₹95–100 Crore**. Against share capital of only ₹3.69 Cr and reserves of ~₹42 Cr, the accumulated losses destroyed shareholder equity entirely.

**Resolution:** The Nagpur slump sale (₹22.60 Cr) and gradual loss reduction improved net worth to +₹4.1 Cr in FY25 — but FY26 losses pushed it back to -₹5.2 Cr.

**Key question for management:** What is the full quantum of accumulated losses and when does the company project to fully restore positive net worth?

### Flag 3: PSU Receivable Provision — SEVERITY: HIGH
