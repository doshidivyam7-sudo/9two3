# Tata Consultancy Services Limited (TCS.NS) - First Look

**Verdict:** DEEP-DIVE &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Conviction:** 3/5 &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Screen score:** 67.5

> 48% ROE, net cash and 15x earnings: the market prices structural AI decline. A deep-dive should test whether that is overdone.

## Why it screened
Best ROE in the hit list (47.7%; ROCE 54.9%), negligible debt (D/E 0.1), and the lowest P/E of any hit (15.4x). But the 13.9% revenue growth that cleared the 8% bar is **mostly the rupee**. Q1 FY27 revenue of ₹72,275 cr was up 13.9% in INR, but only **3.2% YoY in constant currency** (+0.4% QoQ). The rupee hit a record ~95.4/$ in May 2026. Earnings grew 4.6%, and the 3-year revenue CAGR is 5.8%. Strip out currency and this is a low-single-digit grower. It passes the growth filter on a technicality.

## What the business does
India's largest IT services firm (₹2.67 lakh cr FY26 revenue). It runs application development and maintenance, infrastructure, consulting and BPO for global enterprises, with BFSI and North America the largest exposures. It is controlled by Tata Sons.

## What looks good
- Returns and cash: ROE near 48%, no leverage, 3.1% dividend yield. It has a long record of returning most of its cash through dividends and buybacks.
- Deal flow has not collapsed. Q1 TCV was $9.5bn, and the company reports an AI revenue run-rate of about $2.6bn.
- Margins held: FY26 EBIT of ₹66,714 cr was roughly flat on revenue up 4.6%, after the labour-code and restructuring charges.

## What could be wrong
- **AI deflation is the whole debate.** IT stocks sold off after the February 2026 Claude Code roll-out, and again in June. TCS hit its 52-week low of ₹1,976.80 on 1 July 2026. If AI compresses billable effort faster than it creates new work, 3% constant-currency growth goes to zero or below. That would make 15x a fair multiple, not a cheap one.
- **Governance overhang at the parent.** N Chandrasekaran will not seek reappointment as Tata Sons chairman when his term ends on 20 Feb 2027, after a boardroom split with Noel Tata. TCS fell 4.1% on the news. The mandate avoids governance risk, so this needs a view.
- **Legal:** a ₹668 cr exceptional charge in Q1 to settle the CSC claim, after the US Supreme Court denied TCS's petition.
- Workforce cut of ~12,000 (2%) announced in FY26. Watch for further restructuring charges.
- Data quirk: Yahoo shows a 46.5% payout, which looks low against TCS's history. Verify before leaning on yield support.

## Valuation sanity check
At 15.4x trailing (13.1x forward per Yahoo), the earnings yield is ~6.5%, plus a 3.1% dividend. That roughly implies flat-to-low-single-digit long-run growth. Price is ₹2,118: 36.8% below the 52-week high and down 30.6% over 12 months. It is cheap against its own history and against peers in this screen (HCL 18.9x, TechM 26.6x). It is only cheap in absolute terms if constant-currency growth stays positive.

## Recent developments
- Q1 FY27 (9 Jul 2026): revenue +13.9% INR / +3.2% CC; net profit ~₹13,349 cr (+4.6%) after the CSC charge; ₹12 interim dividend.
- Sep 2026: Tata Sons chairmanship uncertainty; stock trading around ₹2,090.

## Questions a deep-dive must answer
1. Revenue mix at risk: what share is run/maintain and testing work that AI compresses, versus transformation work?
2. Is the AI run-rate incremental, or a relabelling of existing work? What is AI's deflation drag on renewals?
3. Who succeeds Chandrasekaran, and does Tata Sons' cash need change TCS's capital allocation?
4. Constant-currency growth outlook by vertical for FY27–28, ex-rupee.
5. Any further CSC/DXC or other litigation exposure?
