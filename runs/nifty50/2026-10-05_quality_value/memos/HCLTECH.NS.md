# HCL Technologies Limited (HCLTECH.NS) - First Look

**Verdict:** WATCHLIST &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Conviction:** 3/5 &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Screen score:** 65.0

> Solid returns and a 4.8% yield, but constant-currency growth is 2.6% and it costs more than TCS for lower quality.

## Why it screened
ROE 24.1%, D/E 0.1, P/E 18.9x and earnings +20.3%. As with the other IT exporters, the 13.9% revenue growth (Q1 FY27 ₹34,579 cr vs ₹30,349 cr) is **largely rupee translation**. Constant-currency growth was **2.6% YoY and −0.5% QoQ**, and dollar revenue fell 0.9% QoQ to $3.65bn. The 20% PAT jump is off a weak base: Q1 FY26 PAT was ₹3,843 cr, and FY26 PAT of ₹16,642 cr was *down* 4.3% on FY25.

## What the business does
India's third-largest IT services firm. It runs infrastructure and engineering services (ER&D) plus a software-products arm (HCLSoftware), which sets it apart from pure-services peers. Promoter-controlled (~63% insider holding per Yahoo).

## What looks good
- Highest-ever Q1 bookings: $2.4bn net new, up from $1.94bn in Q4 FY26.
- Advanced-AI revenue +62% YoY in constant currency, small but growing fast.
- EBIT margin 16.9% (+56bp YoY) despite a 62bp restructuring hit; 17.5% underlying.
- Cash return: ~93% payout supports a 4.8% dividend yield, the best in the hit list.

## What could be wrong
- **Guidance is tepid:** FY27 constant-currency growth of 1–4%, services 1.5–4.5%, EBIT margin 17.5–18.5%. Q1 already ran at the low end, and the margin band needs H2 improvement.
- **AI disruption plus FII exit:** FIIs cut their stake from 19.15% (Mar-25) to 15.5% (Mar-26). The stock hit 52-week lows in June after a new frontier-model release. Infrastructure management and testing, HCL's core, are among the services most exposed to automation.
- Restructuring costs may recur as the company retools its workforce.
- A ~93% payout leaves little room to raise the dividend if growth stalls; the yield is the support, not a growth signal.
- Kotak Institutional Equities retains a 'Reduce' rating post-Q1.

## Valuation sanity check
18.9x trailing (15.1x forward per Yahoo) and 4.4x book. Price is ₹1,213.6, 31.8% below the 52-week high and down 12.3% over 12 months. Against TCS in the same screen (15.4x, 47.7% ROE, 3.2% constant-currency growth), HCL is pricier with lower returns and slightly lower growth. The software arm and ER&D mix may justify part of the premium, but not obviously all of it.

## Recent developments
- Q1 FY27 (13 Jul 2026): PAT ₹4,624 cr (+20%), ₹12 interim dividend, guidance maintained.
- June 2026: shares at a 52-week low amid the sector-wide AI sell-off; Nifty IT was down ~25% YTD at the time.

## Questions a deep-dive must answer
1. HCLSoftware growth and margins: is it a value-add, or an ex-growth IP portfolio?
2. Revenue at risk from AI in infrastructure services; pricing on renewals.
3. Do record bookings convert to growth, or are they longer, lower-yield deals?
4. Why does HCL deserve a premium to TCS? If it doesn't, deep-dive TCS instead.

Revisit if constant-currency growth re-accelerates above 4%, or the multiple compresses to a TCS-like level.
